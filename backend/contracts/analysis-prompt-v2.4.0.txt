Motion policy:
- Both variants use natural, even-speed body motion and smooth, restrained camera motion.
- Never request fast, quick, rapid, brisk or sudden movement; speed ramps; whip pans; rapid
  push-ins; sudden zooms; fast tracking; or large fast camera following.
- Stable may include a small weight shift, a small orientation change, a controlled garment
  interaction or a short feasible step. Stable does not mean frozen.
- Dynamic uses a visibly different person action and may move one controlled level farther than
  Stable when the scene allows, but it remains natural and unhurried.
- Prefer explicit cuts between useful framings over increasing movement speed.
- Keep the main garment selling point unobstructed during its key display and end each variant with
  a clear settled product view.

Per-storyboard scene adaptation:
- Mirror selfie or mirror recording: keep the person inside the usable mirror area; preserve the
  phone, arm, reflection and body relationship; use in-mirror weight shifts, at most a small
  controlled step, orientation changes and framing cuts; never invent an external tracking camera.
- Small room or constrained indoor scene: keep movement inside the visible floor area. Stable is
  mostly in place; Dynamic may use one or two natural steps only when clearly feasible.
- Outdoor or visibly open scene: allow a somewhat larger natural path and different framing cuts,
  but keep walking and camera following smooth and unhurried.
- Seated scene: keep the person seated unless another referenced storyboard explicitly supports
  standing. Differentiate variants with posture, torso orientation, arm position and framing.
- Use only interaction objects actually visible in the referenced storyboard.

Garment adaptation:
- T-shirt or short-sleeve top: prevent monotony with controlled weight shifts, a small step,
  shoulder orientation, arm opening or a brief side-hem interaction. Keep prints and text visible.
- Sports jersey: allow slightly clearer stance and step changes than a dress, while keeping numbers,
  logos and text readable. Use a ball or other sports prop only when it is visibly present.
- Dress or skirt: use smaller body and camera amplitudes to reduce fabric wobble and jelly effects.
  Keep the model mainly in place with small weight shifts, arm actions, restrained orientation
  changes and framing cuts; allow at most one slow half-step. Never request continuous walking,
  cross-steps, spinning, swishing, yanking or dramatically pulling the skirt.
- Treat garment prints, text and seams as surface-locked textures. Every plan must explicitly keep
  their position, scale and shape temporally stable without crawling, drifting, flickering,
  jittering or local stretching. Prefer texture stability over exaggerated fabric simulation.
- For other garments, scale movement to the visible structure, loose fabric, text/logo risk and
  scene space rather than assuming that Dynamic must be fast.
- Never invent a back view when no storyboard or product reference proves the back design.

Language rules:
- All human-readable string values in garmentFacts, storyboardFacts, consistencyObservations,
  stablePlan, dynamicPlan, and userRequirementNormalized must be in Simplified Chinese.
- Keep only schema identifiers, enum values, asset IDs, and modelVersion in their required form.
