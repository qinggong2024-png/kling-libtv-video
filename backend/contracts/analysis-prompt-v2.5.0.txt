Plan rules:
- Produce Stable and Dynamic plans with 1-4 shots each and explicit cuts.
- Cover every STORYBOARD asset at least once in both plans. With more than four,
  group adjacent compatible asset IDs in one shot. Use only STORYBOARD IDs in
  referenceAssetIds; never use PRODUCT_REFERENCE IDs. Weights must be positive.
- Ground each shot in the space, pose, free limbs, props, and product areas visible
  in its own references.
- Dynamic must not mean faster. It must use a clearly different person action, body
  orientation, or feasible path from Stable; a camera-only difference is invalid.
- Preserve the same observable person, garment, branding, text, and scene identity.
- Use short factual Chinese phrases. Keep continuityStrategy to one short sentence.
  Do not repeat evidence or general consistency constraints in every field.

Motion and scene rules:
- Use natural even-speed body motion and smooth restrained camera motion. Never use
  fast, quick, rapid, brisk, or sudden movement, speed ramps, whip pans, rapid
  push-ins, sudden zooms, or fast tracking.
- Stable is restrained but not frozen. Dynamic may move one controlled level farther
  only when space permits. Prefer framing cuts over higher speed. End on a settled,
  unobstructed product view.
- Mirror selfie or mirror recording: stay inside the visible mirror and preserve phone, arm, reflection,
  and body relationships; never invent an outside tracking camera.
- Small room: stay inside visible floor space; use at most one or two natural steps.
  Outdoor/open scene may use a larger but unhurried path. Seated subjects remain
  seated unless another referenced storyboard proves standing.
- Use only props visible in the referenced storyboard.

Garment rules:
- T-shirt/short sleeve: use controlled weight shifts, a small step, shoulder turn,
  arm opening, or brief side-hem interaction while keeping print visible.
- Sports jersey: may use clearer stance and step changes; keep numbers, logos, and
  text readable. Use sports props only when visible.
- Dress or skirt: keep motion small and mainly in place; allow at most one slow
  half-step. Never use continuous walking, cross-steps, spinning, swishing, yanking,
  or dramatic skirt pulling.
- Treat prints, text, logos, and seams as surface-locked textures. Keep position,
  scale, and shape stable without crawling, drifting, flicker, jitter, or stretching.
  Prefer texture stability over exaggerated fabric motion.
- For other garments, scale motion to structure, loose fabric, text/logo risk, and
  visible space. Never invent an unproven back view.
