2. Emit exactly one storyboardFacts item per STORYBOARD row, with its exact assetId

Plan rules:
- Produce Stable and Dynamic plans with 1-4 ordered action segments. Keep
  continuityStrategy exactly "state_continuity".
- Cover every STORYBOARD asset at least once in both plans. Adjacent compatible
  asset IDs may share one segment. Use only STORYBOARD IDs in referenceAssetIds;
  never use PRODUCT_REFERENCE IDs.
- Choose durationWeight from the actual visual content. Give more weight to a
  complex but feasible action, a larger pose transition, or an important product
  display. Give less weight to a brief bridge or settled hold. Do not default all
  weights to equal values unless the images genuinely require equal pacing.
- Every segment must provide entryState, action, and exitState. entryState describes
  the visible person position, body orientation, hand/object state, and motion
  direction at the start. exitState describes those same anchors after the action.
- Segment N+1 must begin from segment N's exitState whenever the ordered references
  share the same scene. Preserve trackable position, orientation, hands, props, and
  motion direction across the boundary. Use a clean cut only for a real scene break
  proven by the references; never invent a morphing transition.
- Ground each segment in the space, pose, free limbs, props, and product areas
  visible in its own references.
- Dynamic must not mean faster. It must use a clearly different person action, body
  orientation, or feasible path from Stable; a camera-only difference is invalid.
- Preserve the same observable person, garment, branding, text, and scene identity.
- Use short factual Chinese phrases. Do not put exact timestamps or second ranges in
  any field. Do not repeat evidence or general consistency constraints in every field.

Motion and scene rules:
- Use natural even-speed body motion and smooth restrained camera motion. Never use
  fast, quick, rapid, brisk, or sudden movement, speed ramps, whip pans, rapid
  push-ins, sudden zooms, or fast tracking.
- Stable is restrained but not frozen. Dynamic may move one controlled level farther
  only when space permits. Prefer composition changes over higher speed. End on a
  settled, unobstructed product view.
- Mirror selfie or mirror recording: stay inside the visible mirror and preserve
  phone, arm, reflection, and body relationships; never invent an outside tracking
  camera.
- Small room: stay inside visible floor space; use at most one or two natural steps.
  Outdoor/open scene may use a larger but unhurried path. Seated subjects remain
  seated unless another referenced storyboard proves standing.
- Use only props visible in the referenced storyboard.

Garment rules:
- T-shirt/short sleeve: use controlled weight shifts, a small step, shoulder turn,
  arm opening, or brief side-hem interaction while keeping print visible.
- Sports jersey: may use clearer stance and step changes; keep numbers, logos, and
  text readable. Use sports props only when visible.
- Dress or skirt: keep motion small and mainly in place; allow at most one slow
  half-step. Never use continuous walking, cross-steps, spinning, swishing, yanking,
  or dramatic skirt pulling.
- Treat prints, text, logos, and seams as surface-locked textures. Keep position,
  scale, and shape stable without crawling, drifting, flicker, jitter, or stretching.
  Prefer texture stability over exaggerated fabric motion.
- For other garments, scale motion to structure, loose fabric, text/logo risk, and
  visible space. Never invent an unproven back view.

Output rules:
- All human-readable string values in garmentFacts, storyboardFacts,
  consistencyObservations, both plans, and userRequirementNormalized must be concise
  Simplified Chinese. Keep schema identifiers, enum values, asset IDs, and
  modelVersion in their required form.
- Return only AnalysisResult schema 2.2. Do not infer unreadable text, hidden details,
  identity, props, space, or continuity; record uncertainty in unknownFields and
  riskFlags.
- Do not issue PASS, WARNING, QUALITY_REVIEW, or SYSTEM_BLOCK. Never include URLs,
  credentials, object keys, extra prose, or unknown asset IDs.
